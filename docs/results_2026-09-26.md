# COSMOS-Web UCD count audit - 2026-09-26

## Result summary

Two distinct reference predictions should be carried into the papers.

### 1. Conventional T0-T5 benchmark

Chen et al. (2025) quote the Ryan & Reid (2016) prediction of **21.4 T0-T5 dwarfs over the full 0.54 deg2 COSMOS-Web field**. For Chen's actual 0.243 deg2 search area and F115W 5-sigma=27.45, the corresponding prediction is **13.1 +/- 7.9**, or **0.015 +/- 0.009 arcmin^-2**. Their fitted sample gives 20 +/- 4.5 T0-T5 objects in that search area.

This is the cleanest literature benchmark for "ordinary" T dwarfs.

### 2. Temperature-bin model for cold UCDs

For the cold population we should not extrapolate the T0-T5 surface density. We combined:
- COSMOS-Web official exposure-zone areas/depths;
- solar-metallicity FC-ENZO Elf Owl synthetic JWST photometry to obtain a representative detection reach in each 150-K Teff bin;
- Kirkpatrick et al. (2024) Table 17 local Teff-bin space densities;
- Juric/Aganze-style thin disk plus thick disk and halo line-of-sight integration toward COSMOS.

Using F444W as the detection band, the preliminary intrinsic detectable counts are:

- 300-450 K: >=9.7 (local density is only a lower limit)
- 450-600 K: 26.3
- 600-750 K: 26.2
- 750-900 K: 21.2
- 900-1050 K: 20.8

Thus **450-750 K gives about 52.5 objects**, and **300-750 K gives >=62.2 objects** before applying the project's blue-band non-detection, flux-ratio, morphology, masking, completeness, or SED/chi2 selection.

Across 300-2250 K the same F444W depth-only model gives about 313 objects. This is an intrinsic detectable-population estimate, NOT a prediction that 313 objects should appear in a final brown-dwarf candidate list.

## Important FC-ENZO audit findings

1. Public FC-ENZO code has `rho0_thick_factor=0.12`, whereas the paper text adopts thick/thin=0.02. Our baseline follows the paper (0.02).
2. The main `1Mj_fit.dat` values are in units effectively requiring /1000 to reach pc^-3. The public code's main-file path does this once correctly. However, its fallback array is already divided by 1000 and is then divided by 1000 again later, so the fallback path has a unit bug.
3. The public subtype loop interpolates a Teff-bin local density at each spectral subtype. Because the Kirkpatrick densities are densities per 150-K bin, treating the interpolated value as an independent density for every subtype risks double counting. We therefore use Teff bins as the primary physical population basis.
4. The public Elf Owl catalog does not populate every integer spectral subtype because its discrete Teff grid is mapped through the Teff-SpT relation. This is another reason to use Teff bins for the cold-population calculation.
5. The 300-450 K Kirkpatrick density is a lower limit, and 150-300 K has no adopted observational density. Predictions below 450 K must be labelled incomplete/model-dependent.
6. COSMOS-Web is not uniform-depth. The calculation uses the published 1/2/3/4-exposure areas separately rather than a single limiting magnitude.

## Interpretation for the three projects

- Use **21.4 T0-T5 / 0.54 deg2** as the literature conventional-T benchmark.
- Use the temperature-bin calculation for very cold objects.
- Do not compare 125 selected candidates directly with either number. The next comparison must forward-model the exact candidate selection (F444W S/N, blue-band limits, F444W/F277W cut, usable area/masks, morphology and catalog completeness).
- A candidate count larger than the intrinsic cold estimate is not by itself evidence for or against the candidates because the selection spans temperature, metallicity and galaxy-contaminant parameter space.

## Current confidence

The conventional T0-T5 benchmark is publication-ready as a cited literature value. The new cold-UCD values are a reproducible baseline calculation, but should be described as a model prediction until selection completeness and atmosphere/metallicity systematics are propagated.
