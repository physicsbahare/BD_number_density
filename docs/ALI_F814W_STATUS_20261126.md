## Executed checks and implications

The read-only script `src/f814w_input_audit.py` reads gzip FITS headers
without expanding mosaics, records PSF metadata, compares SCI/ERR/WHT WCS
at nine pixels, and compares catalog SNR against AUTO and five aperture
flux/error ratios over deterministic rows distributed across the catalog.
With `--check-weights`, it streams the entire 60 mas A1 ERR/WHT pair in
64-row chunks and samples their numerical relationship. See
`data/f814w_audit_2026-11-26/input_audit.json` for executed results.

The completed run inspected 158 named inputs, including 137 nonempty FITS
files and twenty PSF files. All 82 nine-point SCI/ERR or SCI/WHT header-WCS
comparisons have maximum separation below 1.4e-10 arcsec. This is a
numerical header-parity check, not an absolute astrometric accuracy claim.
The distributed catalog sample contains 60000 rows. For F814W AUTO,
59968 rows pass the documented numerical validity filters; the ratio
`(AUTO flux/error)/stored SNR` has percentiles 0.27911249, 0.43590263,
and 0.76912628 (16th/50th/84th). Only one sampled ratio agrees at rtol=1e-6.

The streamed A1 pair has 119567721 jointly positive finite ERR/WHT pixels.
Among 1232847 deterministically sampled pixels, `ERR² × WHT` has
16th/50th/84th percentiles 1.00742905/1.02514804/1.05547526, with range
1.00000006–952.05183199. Thus WHT is **not exactly reciprocal total ERR
variance**, despite its inverse-flux-squared units. The source of the
excess has not been established here. Neither WHT nor ERR establishes
the correlated aperture noise without empirical calibration.

The A1 SCI/ERR headers specify `BUNIT=nJy`; WHT specifies `1/nJy2`.
The header WCS pixel scales are 0.03 or 0.06 arcsec as named. For A1 at
30 mas, SCI/ERR use PC+CDELT and WHT uses CD. Their actual sky transforms
agree to about 1e-11 arcsec in the initial three-point check. Different
header encodings are not evidence of a WCS bug. The machine-readable
nine-point comparisons extend that check to the available image pairs.
These checks establish header consistency, not absolute registration
against stars or the missing NIRCam depth map grid.

The catalog has 784016 photometry rows. A first-30000-row exploratory check
found no agreement of stored `snr_hst-f814w` with AUTO flux/error at
rtol=1e-6: their ratio's median was 0.43894627. The saved audit uses a
different, explicitly defined distributed sample, so its statistics need
not equal this prefix statistic.

The official [catalog column documentation](https://cosmos2025.iap.fr/catalog.html)
identifies `snr_hst-f814w` as native-resolution significance in a
**0.2 arcsec diameter aperture**. AUTO flux and error instead refer to
corrected elliptical photometry; circular `flux_aper` values are from the
PSF-homogenized aperture catalog. Consequently none of these ratios may
silently replace the stored SNR. The exact native-aperture denominator
must be reproduced and checked against the catalog. Native F814W
apertures, PSF encircled energy, empirical noise, and spatial uncertainty
must be used consistently in the targeted experiment. The discrepancy
is not evidence for a new population model or an arbitrary rescaling.

The [catalog methods paper](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202555799)
describes empirical empty-aperture calibration and distinguishes native
from PSF-homogenized photometry. Its methods support this distinction but
do not replace verification against Ali's exact input catalog.

PSFEx products are spatial polynomial bases, not ready-to-inject 2D arrays.
For A1, PSF_SAMP=0.89594734 image pixels, PSF_FWHM=4.21095228 image pixels,
and PSFAXIS3=3 with a first-degree spatial model. They require evaluation,
resampling, normalization, and validation against the July 2026 science
images before a recovered selection probability can be accepted.
